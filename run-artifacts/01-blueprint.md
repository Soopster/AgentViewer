# Poem Blueprint

This is the shared creative spec for the test-run poem. All stanza writers, the epigraphist, title crafter, and critic must honor it exactly.

## 1. Theme & mood

- **Theme:** The quiet aftermath of a departure — what remains in a room (or a landscape) once someone has left. Absence made almost physically present.
- **Mood:** Meditative, gently melancholic, but warm rather than bitter. A sense of looking back without regret.

## 2. Tone

Quiet, plainspoken, slightly formal. The speaker observes concrete details (a chair, dust, light on a floorboard) rather than stating feelings directly. Restraint over confession; suggestion over announcement.

## 3. Meter & rhyme scheme

- **Meter:** Iambic pentameter, kept flexible — perfect beats are encouraged but a foot may be dropped or an extra unstressed syllable added in any line where it serves the ear.
- **Rhyme scheme:** Unrhymed blank verse (no end rhymes). Internal sound-play (assonance, alliteration) is welcome; end-rhyming is not.

## 4. Structure

- **Three stanzas**, each **5 lines** long, in the order: opening -> development -> resolution.
- Stanza 1 opens the scene and sets a quiet tension (something is missing).
- Stanza 2 deepens/complicates the feeling — adds a detail or turn that complicates the opening.
- Stanza 3 resolves — lands the poem by echoing the theme to a soft close.

## 5. Keyword palette

Stanza writers must work in at least **one** of these: `window`, `dust`, `floorboard`, `chair`, `light`, `name`, `late`, `doorway`.

## 6. Word budget

Roughly **8-14 words per line**. No line under 6 words; none over 16. Keep each line a single readable breath.
