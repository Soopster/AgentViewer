# What the Room Remembers

*on the room you left*

> Parting is all we know of heaven,
> And all we need of hell.
>
> — Emily Dickinson

## Stanza 1

The chair against the wall still holds its shape,
an empty hollow where your body leaned.
Dust climbs the window where the late light spills,
a drift of silence on the floorboard grain,
and nothing but your name still in the room.

## Stanza 2

Your chair still holds a hollow of your heat,
and dust, since you left, settles without hurry
across the floorboards; light leans in the window
and finds the place your name no longer answers to,
a doorway swallows every footfall late for home.

## Stanza 3

The hollow where you leaned will hold the dark,
the dust will find the track of the last late light,
and when the door swings shut on its own weight
the name you left will keep the careful quiet —
not grief to lose, but stillness left to keep.

---

## Companion reflection

The poem is about the quiet aftermath of a departure — absence made almost physical, felt through the concrete furniture of an empty room. Its subject is not the leaving itself but what remains afterward: the hollow in a chair, the unhurried settling of dust, the lingering weight of a name. The mood is meditative and warm rather than bitter; the speaker looks back without accusation.

The meter serves this theme directly. Flexible iambic pentameter gives the lines the feel of regular, deliberate breath, while the unrhymed blank verse keeps the effect plainspoken — no sonic flourish distracts from the restraint the blueprint asks for. Each stanza is five lines, allowing a controlled arc: the scene opens, complicates, then resolves into stillness rather than grief.

A craft note: the poem keeps its imagery local and concrete throughout, letting objects carry emotion the speaker never names outright. That restraint — saying the absence through a chair and a threshold instead of declaring it — is what keeps the melancholy warm, and the closing gesture of stillness stands on the strength of those quiet details rather than on announcement.
